Model and scope

The amplifier obeys dV/dt + V/τ = −I/Cf, with τ = Rf Cf. It starts at zero relative to the DC baseline. The calculation integrates the average current exactly within each time bin. Changing capacitance at fixed discharge time also changes the implied resistance. The ideal-integration option has no feedback discharge.

The ion kernel assumes a central normal trajectory toward a square pad surrounded by an infinite grounded conductor. It covers only the finite gap: its charge is e[1 − φw(gap)], without rescaling to one elementary charge. Gaussian packets use thermal bulk diffusion, mapped to arrival time with the bulk drift speed, followed by identical final trajectories. Uniform and double-packet cases are diagnostics; none is an event-ensemble or IFG-focusing prediction.

Finite amplifier gain, input capacitance, bandwidth, nonlinear MOS discharge, leakage, saturation, noise, power and stability require a further circuit model or SPICE. No values for these are inferred from this calculation. The output is unclipped and may exceed the range of a real amplifier.
