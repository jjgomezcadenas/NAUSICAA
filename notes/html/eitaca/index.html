eITACA

The electron-reading ITACA detector in xenon–helium (also ITACAe, IE), without ion readout. Default set: variant IE_L1500_D400; user design inputs, 2026-11-17.

Pilot

eITACA pilot: does the SiPM readout saturate? bb0nu and single electrons of the Xe/He pilot, with the eITACA optics and, as reference, the optics and yield of the hybrid production.

EL slope: the paper gives the slope of the mixtures equal to pure xenon within 10 % and the 15.8 % threshold 1.083 ± 0.007 kV/cm/bar (fit above 1.3 kV/cm/bar). The slope 134 follows from the measured yield ratio to pure xenon at 1.6 kV/cm/bar, 0.64 ± 0.01, with pure xenon at Y/p = 140 E/p − 116 (Monteiro et al., JINST 2 (2007) P05001, the normalisation of the paper): 0.64 × 108 / (1.6 − 1.083) = 134. The pure-xenon slope 140 would give 4.5 % more light.

Drift: 60 kV over 150 cm, 400 V/cm. EL: 15 kV over 5 mm, 30 kV/cm = 1.875 kV/cm/bar, 849 photons per electron. The drift ends at the gate: no ion plane and no transfer gap.

Optics without light guides

Blue photons entering the SiPM per EL photon (EItaca.direct_light_acceptance):

acceptance = axial_light_acceptance(r, d) × TPB conversion × forward fraction
           = 0.1096 × 0.65 × 0.5 = 0.0356
  1. VUV emission isotropic and uniform along the hole axis; absorbing hole walls (axial_light_acceptance, src/event_analysis/grid_readout.jl; derivation in docs/elm.tex).
  2. The SiPM sits against the hole exit and its 3 mm square covers the 2.5 mm hole: every VUV photon leaving the hole reaches the TPB on the SiPM window.
  3. TPB converts with probability 0.65 and re-emits isotropically; half of the blue light goes towards the SiPM.
  4. The PDE (0.32) is applied by the SiPM response.

The ITACA module with light guides (elm_light_acceptance) multiplies the same factors by the guide capture 1 − n_gap/n_guide = 1/3. Without guides the light per EL photon is three times larger (test tests/eitaca/test_eitaca.jl). With the eITACA yield (849 photons per electron) the SiPMs see 9.68 photoelectrons per drift electron.
