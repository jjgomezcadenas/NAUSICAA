The three readout stages

Simulation of the ion and EL readout in three stages: detector, electronics, reconstruction.

Two tracks, three stages

An event produces ionisation electrons and ions. These result in an ion track (itrack) and an electron track (etrack). The itrack is read with an array of bare metallic pads (TopMetal) connected to a charge-sensitive amplifier (CSA). The etrack is amplified by EL light production and read by SiPMs connected to custom ASIC electronics. The response of the detector to the itrack and the etrack is simulated in three stages, and each stage saves an HDF5 file that the next one reads:

  1. Detector: drift, diffusion and collection. Output: itrack ions per pixel and etrack photons per SiPM, per time bucket.
  2. Electronics: CSA and ADC for the itrack; PDE, SiPM and ASIC for the etrack. Output: digitised signals.
  3. Reconstruction: charge, energy and tracks from the digitised signals.

Both tracks come from the same sampled event, so the detector or electronics of one can change without resampling the other. Each stage has its own configuration card and calibration. The Monte Carlo truth is kept apart from the saved data and used only for validation.

Photons are saved before the PDE, which is applied in stage 2 with the SiPM.
Stage 1

Detector: transport and collection

Ions

itrack: ions at each pixel

Drift and diffuse the ions of the sampled track and assign each to a pixel and an arrival-time bucket.

Output
Integer ion counts per pixel and bucket, with geometry, timing, event number and provenance.
EL

etrack: photons at each SiPM

Drift and diffuse the electrons, apply attachment, route them to the EL holes, produce the EL light and propagate it to the SiPMs.

In the present ELM the saved photons are blue, after TPB and the light guides.

Output
Integer photon counts per SiPM and bucket, with the photon band, geometry, timing and provenance.

A change of pixel pitch, EL field or optics requires rerunning stage 1. A change of PDE does not.

Stage 2

Electronics

Ions

itrack: CSA and ADC

  1. Turn the arriving ions into the induced current.
  2. Apply the calibrated CSA response, including the charge already on the feedback and its discharge.
  3. Add the correlated voltage noise.
  4. Sample and digitise with the ADC.
Output
ADC counts per acquired pixel (compressed UInt16), sample times, range and calibration identifier.

Initial settings: 100 µs sampling, 12 bits, input range 0–1.8 V (provisional). A 14-bit run is kept for comparison.

EL

etrack: PDE, SiPM and ASIC

  1. Detect each photon with probability PDE (binomial).
  2. Apply microcell occupancy and recovery, and dark counts.
  3. Convert the avalanche charge with the ASIC response, with its noise and current limit.
Output
Digitised charge per SiPM and bucket in photoelectron (PE) equivalents, with timing, calibration and range.

Current settings: PDE 0.32, 250 ns buckets, 1 mA ASIC limit, 0.5 PE RMS noise per bucket; all configurable.

One PE equivalent is the charge of an avalanche in a fully recovered microcell; a partly recovered microcell gives less. The ASIC output is already digital, so the etrack needs no separate ADC model.

Simulation truth, such as which samples clipped, is saved apart from the data and used only for validation.

Stage 3

Reconstruction

Ions

itrack: ADC counts → charge and track

Estimate the CSA state from the baseline before the ions arrive, filter the samples, and invert the calibrated linear or nonlinear response to obtain the arriving charge per bucket.

The subsequent hit builder converts reconstructed ion vectors to itrack hits and sums the event charge. Track finding comes later.

Output
Signed reconstructed ion vectors, uncertainties and quality information.
EL

etrack: ASIC output → charge and track

Apply pedestal, gain and channel calibration; correct for SiPM recovery where the correction is well defined; correct for PDE, optics and attachment.

The subsequent hit builder converts reconstructed PE vectors to etrack hits and sums the event PEs. Saturated observations carry the corresponding quality information.

Output
Reconstructed PE vectors, uncertainties and quality information.

The etrack measures the energy. The itrack gives the topology and a cross-check of the total charge. Both use the same coordinates and event number.

A new filter or inversion reruns only stage 3, on the saved stage 2 files. Julia does the analysis; Python makes figures, tables and HTML reports from the saved results. LaTeX is reserved for physics notes.

What each stage reads and configures

StageReadsIts card sets
1 · DetectorSource Monte Carlo hits and event positionsGas, fields, drift, diffusion, lifetime, grids, optics and detector time buckets
2 · ElectronicsStage 1 HDF5; acquisition specification; device calibrationCSA, ADC, PDE, SiPM recovery, ASIC, noise and sampling

Every file records event numbers, carrier (ion or electron), gas, channel geometry, coordinate convention, time origin, units, input file hashes, cards, seeds and calibration identifiers. Ion and EL files are separate; the production card names the matched pair.

ADC sampling period, drift velocity and bucket width are three different quantities and are recorded separately. The drift field is 300 V/cm at 15 bar (20 V/cm/bar). The bucket width in mm follows from the velocity; older files keep the conversion they were made with.

The ion collection potentials are 8/3/0 kV, as in the manuscript.

Assumed trigger inputs

Fast-trigger implementation is postponed. The run supplies the accepted event numbers, acquisition region and ion-arrival window as explicit inputs.

The operating design accepts EL energy within Qββ ± 1 FWHM and predicts the itrack position and arrival interval. This is described in the physics note.

The ion ADC record includes a pre-arrival baseline, the signal interval and a short post-arrival recording to finish the filter.
