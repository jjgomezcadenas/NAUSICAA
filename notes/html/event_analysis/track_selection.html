Track selection on the readout lattice

Which accepted events are kept as one track, and why.

ITACA index · results: selection on bb0nu, single electrons and ²¹⁴Bi

Purpose

A bb0nu event is one continuous track. The backgrounds at Qbb are gammas of ²¹⁴Bi and ²⁰⁸Tl, and a gamma event rarely leaves one track alone:

  • A photoelectric absorption on the xenon K shell emits a K X-ray (29.7 or 33.6 keV) in about three cases of four. At 16 bar the X-ray is absorbed about 1 cm from the start of the track.
  • A Compton scattering leaves the scattered gamma, absorbed elsewhere as a second electron.
  • Bremsstrahlung photons of the track itself give small deposits near it, in signal and background alike.

A deposit detached from the track is therefore a signature of background. The selection keeps the events whose itrack is one track with no detached deposit. The events it keeps go to the classical cut on the endpoint energy and to the network.

The lattice sets the resolution

The itrack is read on pads of 3 mm pitch in frames of 20 ms. With the ion drift velocity of 15.0 cm/s a frame spans 3.0 mm, so a hit is a cell of a cubic 3 mm lattice. Ion diffusion is about 1 mm. A track deposits about 100 keV per centimetre, about 30 keV per cell, well above the 5 keV hit cut: a track is a chain of touching cells.

Connection radiusCells connected
3.5 mmthe 6 cells sharing a face
4.6 mmalso those sharing an edge (18)
5.5 mmall 26 touching cells
8 mmalso cells one or two cells apart: bridges a gap in the track

Two deposits can be told apart when one empty cell lies between them. The radius of 5.5 mm expresses that: touching cells belong together, anything else is separate. A radius of 8 mm hides a deposit up to two cells from the track.

The productions read out before 2026-11-16 used 16.5 cm/s, with frames of 3.3 mm. Their track analysis grouped the hits in 3 mm cells, which left an empty layer every 33 mm in Z and made a radius of 8 mm necessary to keep tracks whole. The components of this page are built from the hits at their own lattice positions and do not have that gap.

Selection

  1. Take the itrack hits above the display cut (ion_hit_cut_keV, 5 keV).
  2. Connect them at ion_segment_connection_mm = 5.5 mm. Each connected set is a component.
  3. A component with at least ion_segment_min_hits = 6 hits is a track segment.
  4. A smaller component with at least ion_deposit_min_keV = 10 keV is a detached deposit. Smaller components, single hits of 5–10 keV, are ignored as noise.
  5. Reject the event if it has a detached deposit.
  6. Accept the event if it has one segment.
  7. With two or three segments (ion_max_segments = 3), connect the hits again at ion_connection_mm = 8 mm. Accept if all segments fall in one component: the track has a short gap. Reject otherwise: segments that stay apart are separate electrons.

Step 7 matters. A long component is not always a piece of the track: in gamma events most second segments are Compton electrons of 100–300 keV, centimetres away. In the samples studied, 40% of the bb0nu events with two or three segments join at 8 mm, against 8% of the ²¹⁴Bi ones.

The endpoints and the endpoint energies E₁ and E₂ of an accepted event are computed on the graph at 8 mm, which is connected by construction.

What it cannot see

A K X-ray absorbed within about 4 mm of a track hit shares cells with the track. No connection radius separates it. About a quarter of the X-rays of photoelectric events are in this case, and another quarter lie at 4–8 mm, where the selection at 5.5 mm finds part of them and a radius of 8 mm found none.

For runs whose feature tables predate these columns:

  1. prepare_track_selection.jl results/readout/<run_label> applies the selection to the hit files and writes track_selection/events.h5.
  2. prepare_second_pass.jl results/readout/<run_label> writes the hits, energy and extent of every component for several radii (second_pass/components_lattice.h5).
  3. xray_truth_distance.py EVENT_LIST OUTPUT.txt NEXUS_FILE... gives, for gamma events, the Monte Carlo distance of the K X-ray to the main track.
  4. present_track_selection.py OUTPUT_DIR SIGNAL_RUN BACKGROUND_RUN... [--truth=RUN_LABEL:TABLE.txt] writes the results page.

Order of the cuts

  1. Energy window of the simulation, fiducial selection and gamma veto (fast trigger).
  2. ROI cut: reconstructed electron energy within one FWHM of Qbb. It removes most of the ²¹⁴Bi line, which lies 10 keV below Qbb, and ²⁰⁸Tl events more strongly still.
  3. Track selection of this page.
  4. Separation of one electron from two: the cut on E₂ or the network.
