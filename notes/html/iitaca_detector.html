iItaca detector and operating conditions

The ion-reading version of ITACA in pure xenon (iItaca, also written ITACAi or II): common design values, the two variants II_L1000_D500 and II_L1500_D350, and the quantities derived from them.

PrincipleCommon design valuesThe two variantsDerived quantitiesSimulationOpen itemsSources

Design values: user design inputs of 2026-11-17. The detector cards of iItaca and the ion-only simulation mode are to be written from this page. Layout and formulas follow the ITACA detector page; the derived values are computed with work/iitaca/iitaca_numbers.py.

1. Purpose and principle

  1. The ions give the track. The Xe2+ molecular ions (written Xe2+, charge +e) drift to the TopMetal pads with a thermal spread of 1.0 to 1.5 mm and are read on a cubic 3 mm lattice.
  2. The electrons give the start time, the fast trigger and the event energy. They drift to the EL plane, read by SiPMs at 8 mm pitch.
  3. The gas is pure xenon enriched in Xe-136, without helium. The electron spread at full drift is about 10 mm, so the electron plane gives a coarse image and the track topology comes from the ions.
  4. The simulation covers the ion side: Geant4, ion transport, VH1 electronics, reconstruction, hits and tracks. The electron side is emulated: the fast trigger from the Geant4 deposits and the energy from their sum smeared with the energy resolution.

Ion drift velocity

The reduced mobility K0 = 0.65 cm²/(V·s) of Xe2+ in xenon (Neves et al., via Cortez et al.) and N/N0 = 16.39 for pure xenon at 16 bar, 290 K give v = K0 E N0/N = 19.8 cm/s at 500 V/cm and 13.9 cm/s at 350 V/cm. The adopted 20.0 and 15.0 cm/s make the frames of 15 and 20 ms span exactly 3.0 mm.

5. What the simulation does

Simulated

  1. Geant4 in pure enriched xenon at 16 bar, 290 K, with the geometry of each variant (radius 2.0 m; length 1.0 m or 1.5 m).
  2. Ion transport to the pad plane with the thermal spread of the variant and its ion velocity, on the 3 mm lattice with frames of 15 ms or 20 ms.
  3. VH1 readout with 20 fF capacitors and linear reconstruction of the ion signal (VH1 readout).
  4. Hits and tracks from the reconstructed ion signal.

Emulated

  1. Fast trigger and event frame from the Geant4 deposits (MC fast trigger). The frame is the box of the main track plus 20 mm per side.
  2. Event energy: sum of the Geant4 deposits inside the event frame, smeared with a Gaussian of 0.47 % FWHM at Qββ, scaling as √E.

Order of the cuts

  1. Fast trigger: energy window, fiducial selection, gamma veto on deposits outside the frame.
  2. ROI cut on the emulated energy: 2452.03 to 2463.63 keV.
  3. Track selection on the ion lattice: components at 5.5 mm, detached deposits, segments rejoined at 8 mm (track selection).
  4. Endpoint-energy cut or classification network (classification network).

6. Open items

  • EL module at 8 mm pitch: hole diameter, light-guide dimensions and SiPM size.
  • Electron drift velocity in pure xenon at 16 bar, at 500 and 350 V/cm: a Magboltz run is needed.
  • Electron lifetime.
  • VH1 with 15 ms frames, for II_L1000_D500: check described in docs/handoffs/vh1_frame_15ms.md.

  • Ion mobility: P. N. B. Neves et al., J. Chem. Phys. 133 (2010) 124316, as plotted in A. F. V. Cortez et al., JINST 12 (2017) P06012, Fig. 3; comment in config/detector/drift.toml.

  • Electron diffusion and EL yield in pure xenon: NEXT-DEMO++ paper, papers/xehe_paper.pdf, Table 1 and Section 5.3.
  • Potentials of the ion plane: manuscripts/itaca3/itaca3.tex (IFG at 8 kV, sensors at ground).
  • Energy resolution: full simulation of the Xe/He electron readout. Qββ: M. Redshaw et al., PRL 98 (2007) 053003.
