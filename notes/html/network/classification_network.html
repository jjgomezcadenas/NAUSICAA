Classification network

bb0nu against single electrons from the reconstructed itrack and etrack

PurposeEventsInputTraining fileTypes and functionsArchitectureTrainingMeasuresOutputsCommands

1. Purpose

The network separates bb0nu events (two electrons) from single electrons of the same energy. It reads the reconstructed hits of the itrack (ions, TopMetal pads) and of the etrack (electrons, EL and SiPMs).

The reference is the classical cut on E₂, the smaller of the two endpoint energies of the main track (definition and comparison of two samples).

2. Events used

  1. Start from the events accepted by the production: the selection with a fixed frame, which includes the gamma veto.
  2. Read the feature table of each batch (features.h5, written by prepare_batch_features.jl).
  3. With --roi=COMPARISON.h5, apply the ROI cut: reconstructed electron energy strictly between roi/lower_keV and roi/upper_keV of the comparison file (prepare_species_comparison.jl), compared in double precision.
  4. Keep the events with itrack_selected == 1, the track selection on the readout lattice. Feature tables written before that selection have no such column; for them, keep the events with itrack_components == 1 and etrack_components == 1.

With the ROI cut the file holds the events that reach the cut on E₂ in the selection tables, so the network and the classical cut act on the same events.

The components of a track are counted on the hits above the display cut, with the settings of [track_analysis] in the run card.

Why events without detached deposits

  • The backgrounds are gammas of 214Bi and 208Tl. A detached deposit is a signature of background.
  • A photoelectric absorption emits a xenon K X-ray of about 30 keV, absorbed about 1 cm away.
  • A Compton event leaves separated deposits.
  • Events with detached deposits are rejected before the network. The network addresses the remaining question: one blob or two on a single track.

3. Input

Each track of an event is a 3D grid: the readout lattice inside the fixed frame of 160 mm. Every event has the same grid, so a dense network can be used.

Value of a cellhit energy in keV / 20hit energy in keV / 10
  • The pitches are read from the resolved card saved in the hit file; the frame side from the saved frame of each event.
  • All saved hits of the event are used, noise hits included: this is what the detector delivers, and the network must cope with noise. The saved hits are those above the hit cuts of the hit file (ion_hit_cut_keV, electron_hit_cut_keV).
  • A cell without a hit holds zero. Two hits in one cell are an error.

Assignment of hits to cells

lattice_indices treats each axis separately. Hit coordinates are channel centres, so they lie on a lattice of the given pitch.

  1. Group the coordinates by their position within one pitch. Take as reference the coordinate whose lattice holds most hits.
  2. Give the reference the cell floor((x0 − lower + pitch/2) / pitch), where lower is the lower face of the frame. Cell 0 is centred within half a pitch of that face.
  3. Give every other hit the cell of the reference plus the rounded number of pitches between them.
  4. Stop with an error if a hit is off the lattice by more than the tolerance, or if a cell falls outside 0:cells-1.

The tolerance is 0.02 pitch, and 0.5 pitch on the Z axis of the etrack. The last time group of an event is shorter than the others, and its hits are centred on its actual span; they are assigned to the nearest cell.

Processing one run

  1. List the batch folders batches/bNNNNN of the run.
  2. For each batch, read features.h5 and check that the SHA-256 of hits.h5 is the one recorded in the table.
  3. Check that the settings, the lattice pitches and the frame side are the same in every batch, and that the hit file lists the events in the order of the table.
  4. Select the events of section 2.
  5. For each selected event and each track, read its hits and the lower face of its frame, and call lattice_indices on X, Y and Z.
  6. Append the cell indices, the energies and the feature columns of the event.
  7. Check that no event number is repeated, then write the file. An existing file is preserved.

A 3D residual network. The table gives the grid of each stage with the default first convolution.

Every convolution is followed by batch normalisation and ReLU, and has no bias.

Sparse network

--model=sparse has the stages of stem = "full": the first stage works on the input lattice with a 3×3×3 convolution, and three stride-2 convolutions follow. It computes only on the cells with charge.

  1. Inside a stage, the convolutions keep the set of occupied cells (submanifold convolutions): the charge does not spread into empty cells.
  2. A stride-2 convolution occupies every coarse cell that has an occupied cell in its 3×3×3 input.
  3. Batch normalisation takes its statistics over the occupied cells.
  4. The output vector is the mean over the occupied cells of the last stage.

The itrack grid has 175,616 cells and a median event occupies 157 of them. The parameters are those of stem = "full".

Rationale

  1. Dense and sparse. The frame is fixed and small, so the full grid fits in memory and the dense network runs on any device. At lattice resolution the sparse network gives the same separation in a twentieth of the time (first round: 29 s per epoch against 567 s) and its validation loss has none of the jumps of the dense one. It needs spconv and a CUDA GPU.
  2. Residual blocks. A residual network with stages that reduce the grid and double the channels is the family used in the NEXT topology studies (papers/Next/next_pitch_v5.pdf, built there with sparse convolutions), so results are compared between networks of the same kind.
  3. Three inputs. itrack, etrack and both measure which readout separates better and what the second adds. With both, each track has its own trunk; the two vectors are concatenated and pass through a layer of 64 units.
  4. First convolution. stem = "stride2" (5×5×5) halves the grid at once and is the default: a training step of 64 events takes 0.7 s on the Apple GPU, against 4.3 s for stem = "full" (3×3×3 at lattice resolution, first stage on the input grid). full is kept for GPUs where it is affordable.
  5. Global average. The output does not depend on where the track sits in the frame.

7. Training

scripts/network/network_trainer.py SIGNAL.h5 BACKGROUND.h5 OUTPUT_DIR --input=itrack|etrack|both. The label is 1 for the events of SIGNAL.h5 and 0 for those of BACKGROUND.h5.

  1. With --model=sparse on a machine without spconv or without a CUDA GPU, stop with a message.
  2. Read the two training files and merge them. The two species must differ and the two files must record the same selection.
  3. Split the events by production batch: the batches of --validation-batches and --test-batches are held out, the others train. With --fraction below 1, keep that part of the training events, chosen at random (learning curve).
  4. In each epoch, shuffle the training events and take them in groups of --batch-size. For each group draw one Transform, fill the grids (dense, or sparse tensors) and take one optimisation step.
  5. After each epoch, score the validation events without operations, with the lattice centred, and record the losses and the validation measures.
  6. Keep the weights of the epoch with the lowest validation loss.
  7. Score the test events with those weights and save the results.

Symmetry operations and placement

  • Exchange of X and Y, and reflection of each axis: the eight symmetries of the square in the transverse plane, and the Z reflection.
  • The detector response does not depend on the transverse orientation. Diffusion depends on the drift length of the event, not on the direction of the track.
  • Random placement of the lattice inside the padding of the grid, independently on each axis.
  • The itrack and the etrack of an event receive the same operation and the same placement draw.

Optimisation

  • Loss: binary cross-entropy on the logit.
  • Optimiser: AdamW with --weight-decay.
  • Learning rate: one cycle over all steps, with maximum --learning-rate.

8. Measures

separation(score, label) takes the scores and the labels of a set of events.

  1. Area under the curve. The probability that a signal event scores higher than a background event, computed from the ranks of the scores; tied scores share their mean rank.
  2. Threshold. For each target signal efficiency (90, 80, 70, 60 and 50%), the quantile of the signal scores that keeps that fraction of the signal.
  3. Background acceptance. The fraction of background events with score > threshold, with binomial error √(a(1 − a)/N) for acceptance a and N background events.

The same efficiencies are used for the classical cut on E₂.
